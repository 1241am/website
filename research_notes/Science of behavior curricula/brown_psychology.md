## 1. Degree requirements (A.B./Sc.B., core, tracks, capstone/honors)

### Takeaway
Brown offers both A.B. and Sc.B. Psychology. An older guideline PDF summary gives AB = 12 courses, ScB = 17 courses (with four approved science courses), plus a writing requirement; the current Bulletin summary says five foundation courses, a statistics course (CPSY 0900), research methods, electives/labs/seminars and a capstone. The two sources differ in structure, so exact current counts are UNVERIFIED.

### Cited Findings
- Concentration requires quantitative methods and experimental design courses, familiarity with foundational topics, and "seminars and laboratory courses on specialized topics and state-of-the-art experimental" work — VERIFIED (summary) [Bulletin Psychology](https://bulletin.brown.edu/the-college/concentrations/psyc/)
- AB = 12 courses; ScB = 17 courses; two courses from any dept for writing requirement; gateway CLPS 0010; statistics CPSY 0900 preferred (substitutes APMA 1660, PHP 1501, SOC 1100 with advisor approval); Research Methods CLPS 1900/1901; one foundation in each of Social, Perception/Cognition, Development, Learning/Animal Behavior; four electives (at least two at 1000-level); ScB adds four approved science courses. This is from an OLDER guidelines PDF (uses CLPS prefix) — VERIFIED as content of that older document, possibly superseded [Guidelines PDF](https://copsy.brown.edu/sites/default/files/concentration-docs/CLPS-Psychology-Concentration-Guidelines.pdf)
- Current Bulletin: "five foundation courses, representing core areas of Psychology as a science", all numbered below 1000; approved list on the CPSY undergraduate page — VERIFIED (summary) [Bulletin](https://bulletin.brown.edu/the-college/concentrations/psyc/); [CoPsy concentrations](https://copsy.brown.edu/undergraduate-study/concentrations)
- Foundation subdisciplines: perception, cognition, developmental, behavioral neuroscience, social — VERIFIED (summary) [Bulletin](https://bulletin.brown.edu/the-college/concentrations/psyc/)
- New requirements page exists ("46: The New Psychology Requirements") suggesting a recent revision (possibly 2025 "Standard requirements 2025" PDF) — VERIFIED existence only [CoPsy Advising](https://sites.google.com/brown.edu/clpsadvising/46-the-new-psychology-requirements); [Standard requirements 2025 PDF](https://copsy.brown.edu/sites/default/files/concentration-docs/Standard%20requirements%202025.pdf)
- Capstone: honors program in senior year, OR one semester CPSY 1970 (Directed Readings) or 1980 (Directed Research), OR a CoPsy laboratory course in senior year — VERIFIED (summary) [CoPsy Advising 46](https://sites.google.com/brown.edu/clpsadvising/46-the-new-psychology-requirements). Bulletin summary adds: a senior capstone seminar or independent study with CoPsy, Neuroscience dept, or Carney Institute faculty; honors students may use the second required 1980 as capstone — VERIFIED (summary) [Bulletin](https://bulletin.brown.edu/the-college/concentrations/psyc/)
- Capstone purpose: integrate foundation content with methodological skill from analysis courses — VERIFIED (summary) [CoPsy Advising 46](https://sites.google.com/brown.edu/clpsadvising/46-the-new-psychology-requirements)
- Honors: research project under faculty direction; Statistics and Research Methods must be done before semester 7 — VERIFIED (summary) [Bulletin](https://bulletin.brown.edu/the-college/concentrations/psyc/)
- No formal "tracks" found for Psychology; a concentration-wide structure with foundation areas. Note Brown lists a separate Behavioral Decision Sciences concentration — out of scope [BDS Bulletin](https://bulletin.brown.edu/the-college/concentrations/bds/)

### Inferences
- The AB/ScB counts in the old PDF may be outdated given the 2025 revision; the "five foundation courses" wording is newer than "four foundation topics".
- The ScB likely differs by added science/math courses (from old PDF).

### Gaps

## 2. Prerequisites/foundations; quantitative methods, statistics, design, labs

### Takeaway

### Cited Findings
- CPSY 0900 is required; prerequisite for research methods and laboratory courses; plan by 4th semester — VERIFIED (summary) [Bulletin](https://bulletin.brown.edu/the-college/concentrations/psyc/)

## 10. Hypothesis test: Behavior -> experiment -> measurement -> cognition -> neural mechanism -> computational model

### Takeaway
MODIFY (partially supported). Evidence supports experimental/quantitative emphasis and links to neural and computational levels, but a strictly linear pipeline is not documented; the curriculum is a multi-area (perception, learning, development, social) structure with stats/methods as the shared spine and neural/computational content as electives/labs.

### Cited Findings
- Requirement framing: quantitative methods and experimental design, foundations, lab courses "state-of-the-art experimental" [Bulletin](https://bulletin.brown.edu/the-college/concentrations/psyc/)
- Neural/computational strands exist: behavioral neuroscience, computational models, EEG lab, computational methods course [Focus areas](https://copsy.brown.edu/research/focus-areas); [Bulletin dept](https://bulletin.brown.edu/cognitivelinguisticandpsychologicalsciences/)
- Social and developmental content (moral mind, cooperation, happiness, clinical) sits outside a neural-mechanism chain [Coursicle](https://www.coursicle.com/brown/courses/CPSY/)

### Inferences
- "Experiment -> measurement" supported (stats, methods, labs). "Neural mechanism" and "computational model" supported as options, not requirements for Psychology (the neural emphasis sits in the separate Cog Neuro concentration, not researched here). Modified hypothesis: Brown Psychology treats behavior and cognition as empirically measurable, with required statistical/methods training and optional biological and computational levels of explanation.

### Gaps
- Could not test against syllabi.
