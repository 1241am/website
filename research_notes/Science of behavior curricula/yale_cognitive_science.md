## 1. Degree requirements (core, tracks, quantitative, senior requirement, B.A. vs B.S.)

### Takeaway
Yale offers B.A. and B.S. Cognitive Science: intro course, junior colloquium, a 4-of-6 (listed as 7 rows) breadth requirement, six depth courses from at least two subfields at 3000+ level, one skills course, and a two-term senior requirement (nonempirical essay for B.A.; empirical research for B.S.). There are no formal tracks; students design a DUS-approved depth "theme".

### Cited Findings
- Major = fifteen term courses, 13.5 credits, including intro and senior requirement (VERIFIED via search summary) — [Yale Bulletin, Cognitive Science](https://catalog.yale.edu/ycps/subjects-of-instruction/cognitive-science/)
- Conflict: a different search summary says "fourteen term courses" and lists CGSC 110, CGSC 395 (half credit), "Senior Colloquium CGSC 491" (older numbering, likely an outdated page/roadmap) — [cogsci.yale.edu concentrations page](https://cogsci.yale.edu/the-undergraduate-program/course-concentrations); [B.A./B.S. roadmap PDF listing CGSC 491](https://catalog.yale.edu/ycps/subjects-of-instruction/cognitive-science/CGSC_Roadmap_-_2024-04-01.pdf). Current course-count needs manual confirmation.
- Prerequisite: CGSC 1100. Required: CGSC 3950 Junior Colloquium (survey of contemporary issues/current research; students then choose a senior-essay topic) — [Bulletin](https://catalog.yale.edu/ycps/subjects-of-instruction/cognitive-science/); [search summary of Bulletin courses](https://catalog.yale.edu/ycps/courses/cgsc/)
- Breadth: one course in each of four of six areas. Listed options: Computer Science (CPSC 2010); Economics and Decision Making (ECON 2259); Linguistics (LING 1100, 1160, 1300, 1179, 2320, 2530); Neuroscience (MCDB 3200, NSCI 3400, PSYC 1600 or PSYC 2670); Philosophy (PHIL 1126, 1182, 2269, 2270, 2271); Psychology (PSYC 1100, PSYC S1390, PSYC 1400); Anthropology (ANTH 1482) listed as well (so the summary showed seven entries for "six" areas; discrepancy unresolved) — [Bulletin](https://catalog.yale.edu/ycps/subjects-of-instruction/cognitive-science/)
- Depth: 6 courses in a specific topic/area, from at least 2 subfields, 3000 level or higher; selection must be approved by the Director of Undergraduate Studies to ensure coherence — [Bulletin](https://catalog.yale.edu/ycps/subjects-of-instruction/cognitive-science/)
- Skills (quantitative/methods): 1 course from CPSC 1001, CPSC 2020, LING 2249, PSYC 2670, S&DS 1000, S&DS 2200, S&DS 2300, S&DS S107E, or another with DUS permission — [Bulletin](https://catalog.yale.edu/ycps/subjects-of-instruction/cognitive-science/)
- Senior requirement B.A.: CGSC 4800 (research colloquium leading to topic selection) + CGSC 4810 (nonempirical senior essay). B.S.: CGSC 4900 + CGSC 4910 (empirical research and senior essay) — [Bulletin](https://catalog.yale.edu/ycps/subjects-of-instruction/cognitive-science/); [CGSC course listing](https://catalog.yale.edu/ycps/courses/cgsc/)
- CGSC 1100 is normally taken by end of fall of sophomore year, before admission to the major — [Bulletin CGSC courses](https://catalog.yale.edu/ycps/courses/cgsc/)
- Tracks: no formal tracks; illustrative depth "themes" shown on the program site: Language and Computation (linguistics + CS, NLP informing theories of mind); Social/Moral Cognition (moral beliefs and behavior); Numerical Cognition — [cogsci.yale.edu concentrations](https://cogsci.yale.edu/the-undergraduate-program/course-concentrations)
- Other course examples named on program site: CS (AI, Intelligent Robotics), Linguistics (Evolution of Language, Language Acquisition), MCDB (Neurobiology, Brain Development and Plasticity), Philosophy (Philosophy of Mind, Mathematical Logic), Psychology (Developmental Psychology, Sex, Evolution, and Human Nature) — [cogsci.yale.edu](https://cogsci.yale.edu/the-undergraduate-program/course-concentrations)

### Inferences
- The 6-course depth block + DUS approval makes the major a self-designed interdisciplinary problem-focused program rather than professional training (INFERRED).
- Skills list mixes programming, stats, linguistics-formal methods, and neuroscience methods, so quantitative literacy is required but not a fixed math sequence (INFERRED).

## 10. Hypothesis test
Hypothesis: Yale frames mind/human nature as an interdisciplinary problem integrating brain, cognition, development, evolution, language, philosophy, social behavior, decision-making, computation.
Verdict: CONFIRM with modification (moderate confidence). Support: intro course explicitly integrates psychology, CS, neuroscience, linguistics, philosophy ([Bulletin](https://catalog.yale.edu/ycps/courses/cgsc/)); breadth requires 4 of 6 disciplinary areas incl. economics/decision making and anthropology; depth requires >=2 subfields ([Bulletin](https://catalog.yale.edu/ycps/subjects-of-instruction/cognitive-science/)); program themes include language/computation, moral cognition, numerical cognition; named courses cover development and evolution ([program site](https://cogsci.yale.edu/the-undergraduate-program/course-concentrations)). Modification: the intro course lists five core disciplines (psychology, CS, neuroscience, linguistics, philosophy); evolution, development, social behavior and decision-making appear through electives/depth themes and faculty rather than as required pillars, and the program has no fixed tracks. Psychology is the dominant home department (Scholl, Santos, Keil, Dunham, Chang, Turk-Browne, Jara-Ettinger all in Psychology) (INFERRED).
