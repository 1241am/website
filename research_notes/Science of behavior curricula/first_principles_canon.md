## Q1. Which dependencies in the L0-L11 hypothesis are wrong or incomplete? What revised model is justified?

### Takeaway
The hypothesized stack is a reasonable *teaching order* but not a *dependency order*. Three defects: (1) it is a single linear chain when the real structure is a set of orthogonal axes (Tinbergen's four questions: mechanism, ontogeny, function, phylogeny; Marr's three levels: computation, algorithm, implementation; scale of organization: cell to culture); (2) L0 (epistemology) and L11 (formal models) are cross-cutting toolkits, not bottom/top layers; L2 (evolution) and L8 (development) are explanatory *axes* that apply at every scale; (3) it omits or misplaces consciousness, individual differences, psychopathology, the body/environment (ecological, embodied, enactive), and top-down/feedback causation (culture and cognition reshape neural development).

### Inferences

Specific defects in the hypothesized hierarchy:

1. **L0 is not a layer below biology; it is a method-of-inquiry wrapper.** Measurement, design, statistics, causal inference (Pearl 2009 *Causality*, 2nd ed., CUP [M]; Hernán & Robins 2020 *Causal Inference: What If*, CRC [M]), replication and philosophy of science apply to every level's claims. It should be drawn as an enclosing frame plus a *level-specific measurement theory* (psychophysics at L5, psychometrics for individual differences, phylogenetic comparative methods at L2). Measurement validity is itself a behavioral science topic (Cronbach & Meehl 1955 "Construct validity in psychological tests", Psychol Bull 52:281-302 [M]; Flake & Fried 2020 "Measurement schmeasurement", Advances in Methods and Practices in Psych Science 3:456-465 [M]; Yarkoni 2022 "The generalizability crisis", BBS 45:e1 [M]).
2. **L11 should be split.** (a) A cross-cutting *formal language* (probability, information theory, dynamical systems, decision/game theory, RL) that can describe any level; (b) *model classes* attached to specific levels (e.g., RW/TD at L4, SDT at L5, Bayesian cognition at L6, drift-diffusion at L6/L7, ESS games at L2/L9, cultural-evolution models at L10). Machine learning is both a formal tool and a source of hypotheses (Rumelhart & McClelland 1986 PDP; Mnih et al. 2015 Nature 518:529-533 [M]; Yamins & DiCarlo 2016 Nat Neurosci 19:356-365 [M]).
3. **L2 and L8 are axes, not levels** (Tinbergen). Keep a "Levels of description" axis and a "Four questions" axis; every cell (e.g., "learning x ontogeny", "emotion x phylogeny") is a valid research program.
4. **L3 (neural) is an implementation level for L4-L7, not a prerequisite of them.** Historically, learning theory (Pavlov, Thorndike, RW), psychophysics (Weber-Fechner), and memory (Ebbinghaus) preceded and constrained neuroscience. Marr's argument implies computation-level theories can be developed without L3. Recommend: L3 as "implementation" attached to L4-L7 rather than beneath them; keep as prerequisite only for neuroscience-track students.
5. **L7 (motivation, emotion, reward) is misplaced after L6 (cognition).** Reward/valuation is arguably prior to or co-equal with learning (L4 RL requires a value signal; Thorndike's law of effect; Panksepp 1998 *Affective Neuroscience*, OUP [M]; Berridge & Robinson 1998 Brain Res Rev 28:309-369 [M]). Suggest merging a "valuation/affect" strand across L4-L6 (Damasio 1994 *Descartes' Error* [M]; Barrett 2017 *How Emotions Are Made* [M]; LeDoux 2012 Neuron 73:653-676 [M]). Disagreements about basic emotions (Ekman 1992 Cognition & Emotion 6:169-200 [M]) vs constructionist (Barrett 2006 Perspectives on Psych Sci 1:28-58 [M]) matter here.
6. **L5/L6 boundary is porous.** Attention, memory, and perception are permeated by expectation (predictive processing: Rao & Ballard 1999 Nat Neurosci 2:79-87 [M]; Clark 2013 BBS 36:181-204 [M]). "Information processing" and "cognition" are better as one graded "representation and inference" tier with sensory-to-abstract axis.
7. **L9 (social) is not simply above L8.** Social cognition is developmentally early (Tomasello & Carpenter; Meltzoff & Moore 1977 Science 198:75-78 [M]) and evolutionarily foundational (Dunbar 1998 "The social brain hypothesis", Evolutionary Anthropology 6:178-190 [M]; Humphrey 1976 [M?]). Social/cultural learning shapes L4 (Bandura, Ross & Ross 1961 J Abnormal & Social Psych 63:575-582 [M]) and L6 (Vygotsky 1978 *Mind in Society* [M]). Social should be a cross-cutting *context* that also has its own mechanisms (mirror/mentalizing systems, oxytocin, etc.).
8. **Missing nodes:** consciousness (cross-cutting problem at L3/L5/L6; not a level), individual differences (variance dimension across all levels, links L1 genetics, L2 selection maintaining variation, L8 development, L11 psychometrics), psychopathology (natural experiments and stress tests of mechanisms L3-L8; also an application; Nesse's evolutionary medicine bridges), ecology/environment/body (embodiment), language as a specialized bridging domain (L6/L9/L10), sleep/circadian and interoception (L1/L7), behavioral genetics and epigenetics (L1/L8), methods of *behavioral measurement* (ethogram, operant chamber, psychophysics) as a distinct base layer.
9. **Feedback loops** (bidirectional arrows) to add: L10→L2 (gene-culture coevolution), L9/L10→L8 (parenting/education shape development), L8→L3 (critical periods, plasticity), L7↔L4 (dopamine RPE: reward drives learning; learning re-values stimuli), L6→L3 (top-down attention/control), L3→L1 (behavior alters physiology/epigenome: Meaney 2001 Annu Rev Neurosci 24:1161-1192 [M]; Weaver et al. 2004 Nat Neurosci 7:847-854 [M]), behavior→L2 (Baldwin effect, behavioral drive of evolution: Wyles, Kunkel & Wilson 1983 PNAS 80:4394-4397 [M?]).

**Proposed revised model (summary; expanded in final section):** three-axis structure.
- Frame F0: Scientific method (former L0) + behavioral measurement theory.
- Axis A (scale of organization / mechanism): molecule-cell-circuit (L1/L3) → sensorimotor & learning (L4) → representation & inference (L5+L6) → valuation/affect/motivation (L7, running in parallel with L4-L6) → person (individual differences, consciousness) → dyad/group (L9) → population/culture (L10).
- Axis B (Tinbergen): each node asked 4 ways: mechanism, ontogeny (L8), function, phylogeny (L2).
- Axis C (Marr): each node described computationally, algorithmically, implementationally, with L11 formal models as the shared language.
- Overlay: pathology/variation as stress tests; consciousness as a cross-level puzzle.

## Q3. Which methods belong at each level?

### Takeaway
Methods align with levels but are not exclusive; the key principle is that each method licenses only certain inferences (correlational vs causal, grain of resolution, species). Comparative and formal methods span levels.

### Cited Findings
Table (methods are standard; supporting sources are those named in Q2, tagged [M]):

| Level | Primary methods | Inferential strength / limits |
|---|---|---|
| L0 | RCT, preregistration, power, meta-analysis, DAGs, psychometric validation | Defines causal warrant; Yarkoni 2022 [M] on generalizability |
| L1 | Molecular genetics, twin/adoption, GWAS, pharmacology, endocrinology | Twin designs assume equal environments; candidate-gene failures (Chabris 2012 [M]) |
| L2 | Field observation, ethograms, playback, phylogenetic comparative methods, optimality/ESS modeling, selection experiments | Comparative correlation is not selection; adaptationist storytelling (Gould & Lewontin 1979 [M]) |
| L3 | Single-unit, LFP, EEG/MEG, fMRI, lesions, TMS, optogenetics, calcium imaging | fMRI correlational, reverse inference (Poldrack 2006 [M]); optogenetics/lesion give causal leverage; H.M. lesion evidence |
| L4 | Operant/Pavlovian assays, maze, fear conditioning, computational RL fitting | Species/preparedness limits (Garcia & Koelling 1966 [M]) |
| L5 | Psychophysics (staircase, SDT), RT, eye tracking, ERPs, neuropsychology | Strong quantitative laws (Weber, Stevens) |
| L6 | Reasoning tasks, corpus & judgment data, drift-diffusion, protocol analysis, cognitive modeling, developmental looking time | Task impurity; reliability paradox (Hedge, Powell & Sumner 2018 Behav Res Methods 50:1166-1186 [M]) |
| L7 | Self-report, physiological indices, taste reactivity, microdialysis, voltammetry, economic choice | Construct validity of emotion measures (Barrett et al. 2019 Psychol Sci Public Interest 20:1-68 [M]) |
| L8 | Longitudinal cohorts, deprivation/cross-fostering, critical-period manipulations, infant looking-time, twin | Confounded age/cohort; WEIRD samples (Henrich et al. 2010 [M]) |
| L9 | Confederate experiments, economic games, surveys, IAT, hyperscanning, primate field work | Demand effects; low replicability for subtle priming (OSC 2015 [V]) |
| L10 | Transmission chains, cross-cultural experiments, historical databases, ABMs, natural experiments, networks | Causal inference from observational history is hard; Galton's problem (Mace & Pagel 1994 [M]) |
| L11 | ML/Bayesian fitting, model comparison, parameter recovery, simulation | Models are underdetermined by data; theory-building requires formalization (Guest & Martin 2021 [M]) |

Bibliographic support for the replication anchor: OSC 2015 [V] and other flagged [M] items above.

### Inferences
- A method taxonomy should be by (a) manipulation vs observation, (b) grain (molecular to population), (c) species. This orthogonality further argues against a single linear ladder.

### Gaps
- No source found in this session that provides a formal "method-by-level" matrix; table is synthesis.

## Q5. Proposed revised hierarchy

### Takeaway
Replace the single L0-L11 ladder with (F) a scientific-method frame, (A) a mechanism/scale spine with parallel tracks, (B) Tinbergen's four-question axis applied to every node, (C) Marr's three levels applied to every node, plus (X) cross-cutting problems (consciousness, individual differences, psychopathology, language, social context) and explicit feedback arrows.

### Cited Findings
Design rests on: Tinbergen 1963 / Bateson & Laland 2013 / Nesse 2013 [V]; Marr 1982 [M]; Craver 2007 [M]; Krakauer 2017 [M]; Laland et al. 2000 [M]; OSC 2015 [V]; Schultz et al. 1997 [V]; and the other items cited in Q1-Q4.

**Revised structure**

```
FRAME F: Scientific method & measurement  (former L0)
  F1 Philosophy of science, causal inference, statistics, replication
  F2 Measurement theory & behavioral assays (psychophysics, psychometrics, ethograms)
  F3 Formal language (former L11a): probability, information, dynamics, decision/game theory
     |
AXIS Q (Tinbergen): apply to every node below
  mechanism | ontogeny (former L8) | function | phylogeny (former L2)
AXIS M (Marr): computational | algorithmic (incl. resource-rational) | implementation
     |
SPINE (scale/mechanism, with bidirectional arrows)
  S1 Biological substrate: genes, cells, physiology, homeostasis        (L1)
  S2 Neural implementation: cells, circuits, systems                    (L3, attached as implementation)
  S3 Adaptive change & value:
       S3a Learning/prediction/RL                                       (L4)
       S3b Valuation, motivation, affect, interoception                 (L7; parallel to S3a, S4)
  S4 Representation & inference: sensation, perception, attention,
       memory, concepts, reasoning, language, planning, decision       (L5 + L6)
  S5 Individual: personality, individual differences, self,
       consciousness (problem cutting S2-S4)
  S6 Interaction: social cognition, dyads, groups                       (L9)
  S7 Population: culture, institutions, economies, collective behavior (L10)
  Model classes (former L11b) attach at each S-node
OVERLAYS
  Variation & pathology (RDoC/HiTOP; computational psychiatry): stress-tests every S-node
  Comparative & phylogenetic: L2 methods across species at every S-node
FEEDBACKS
  S7->S1/phylogeny (gene-culture coevolution); S6/S7->ontogeny (parenting, schooling);
  ontogeny->S2 (critical periods); S3b<->S3a (dopamine RPE; value shapes learning);
  S4->S2 (top-down attention); behavior->S1 (epigenetics, stress); behavior->phylogeny (niche construction)
```

Justification of individual moves:
1. F: Meehl 1967, Cronbach & Meehl 1955, Flake & Fried 2020, Yarkoni 2022, OSC 2015 [V] show epistemology and measurement are constitutive of every claim, not preconditions to be finished first.
2. Q axis: Tinbergen 1963/Bateson & Laland 2013 [V]; Mayr 1961 [M].
3. M axis: Marr 1982 [M]; Krakauer 2017 [M]; Griffiths et al. 2015 [M?].
4. Value parallel to learning: RL theory (Sutton & Barto [M]) and Schultz 1997 [V]; Berridge & Robinson 1998 [M].
5. Merge L5+L6: predictive-processing literature (Clark 2013; Rao & Ballard 1999 [M]) and the practical difficulty of separating perception from inference.
6. Individual/consciousness/pathology: see Q1 sources.
7. Social/cultural: Boyd & Richerson 1985; Henrich 2016; Laland et al. 2000; Tomasello 1999; Dunbar 1998 [M].

**Suggested curriculum ordering (pedagogical, not ontological):** F -> S1 -> S2 basics -> S3a -> S4 -> S3b -> Q-axis modules (evolution, development) revisited on each -> S5 -> S6 -> S7 -> advanced formal models; teach F3 formalisms just-in-time.

### Inferences
- The structure is a synthesis; no single published source defends exactly this model. Closest precedents: Tinbergen/Marr multi-axis thinking; Sapolsky's *Behave* chapter structure (seconds to evolution) [M]; Craver's mechanistic levels [M]; RDoC's units-of-analysis matrix (Insel et al. 2010 [M]) which crosses domains with units (genes to behavior) and includes developmental/environment dimensions; the Bateson & Laland "update" [V].
- Possible objections: a 3-axis model is harder to teach; keep the linear order for pedagogy only.

### Gaps
- No empirical test of the hierarchy (e.g., prerequisite structures from actual curricula) was performed; a follow-up should compare with published syllabi and textbook tables of contents, none of which were reachable in this session.
- Publisher pages, SEP, Scholarpedia, Annual Reviews, Google Scholar all inaccessible or unqueried; the majority of citations (tagged [M]) need a Crossref/DOI pass.
